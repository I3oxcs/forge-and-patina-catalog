In 1958 the road circuit at Solitude, in the hills above Stuttgart, was seven miles of
public road closed for a single weekend. A privately entered Porsche 356 needed a wheel
that could shed the heat of its drum brakes through every one of those miles.

The answer was a dished magnesium centre with eight cooling windows, riveted to its rim
and held to the hub by a single central nut. It weighed less than any steel wheel in
the paddock.
