In 1983 German touring cars grew wider, faster and far harder on their brakes. A Cologne
team found that the wheels of the day were trapping the heat inside the barrel.

The answer was drawn in Deutz, across the Rhine from the cathedral: five spokes split in
two, opening the face so the air could pass straight through. Every face was diamond-cut,
so the mechanics could see a crack before it spread.

The Deutz 83 is the youngest wheel in our archive.
